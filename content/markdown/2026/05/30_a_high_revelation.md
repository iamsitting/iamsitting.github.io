The church has seen many real revivals through history, and every one of them matters. People were saved, renewed, and stirred to seek the Lord again. But even with all that, it still feels like God's purpose has not yet been fully reached.

Over the years, through Scripture, ministry, and fellowship with other believers, it helps to look back at how the Lord moved through different generations:

- The Moravian revival (1727), with Count Zinzendorf as a key instrument.
- The First Great Awakening (1730s-1740s), through voices like Jonathan Edwards, George Whitefield, and John Wesley.
- The Welsh revival (1904-1905), often associated with Evan Roberts.
- The Azusa Street revival (1906-1909), with William J. Seymour as a central servant.

None of these started with man, and none ended with man. Remembering them helps keep things thankful and clear-eyed about where things stand today.

After a recent conference on this burden, these points felt freshly real again. It seems clear that God desires a new revival, one of the highest magnitude, and one that would usher in His return.

So the thought that keeps coming back is simple: the revival that is needed has to match the revelation that has been given.

In other words, the quality of revival is limited by the height of revelation.

So what is the highest revelation?

At the highest peak of divine revelation is this: God became man<sup>[1]</sup> so that man may become God<sup>[2]</sup> in life<sup>[3]</sup> and nature<sup>[4]</sup>, but not in the Godhead<sup>[5]</sup>.

This is not a slogan or one more doctrine to collect.

The point is that the highest revival has to match the highest purpose.

There are many important matters in the Christian life. But if there is any hope of entering into a new revival, one thing is crucial: this revelation has to be received deeply. People can only live according to the revelation they have.

If the revelation is only "God forgives sinners," life will stay at that level. If it is only "God empowers believers," life will reflect that too. Both are true and precious, but still not the full view.

The new revival will not come just by recovering outward practices. It will come by recovering God's intention for humanity: a corporate expression of God through transformed human beings.

It is a bit like climbing a mountain.

There is a difference between seeing the peak and arriving at the peak.

To enter the new revival, we must first see, and then we must arrive.

Seeing may mean understanding, agreeing, even feeling stirred for a moment.

Arriving means the vision governs us.

When someone stands on a high peak, everything else is seen from that height. In the same way, when this revelation becomes the governing view, every part of the Christian life is reoriented.

That is what it means to arrive.

## Scripture Support

[1] God became man.
- John 1:1, 14 - The Word was God, and the Word became flesh.
- Colossians 1:15-19 - In Him all the fullness was pleased to dwell.
- Hebrews 1:1-3 - The Son is the exact imprint of God's being.
- Hebrews 2:14-17 - He partook of flesh and blood.

[2] Man may become God in life and nature, but not in the Godhead.
- John 1:12-13 - Those who receive Him become children of God.
- John 3:6 - That which is born of the Spirit is spirit.
- John 10:34-36 - Jesus refers to the Scripture, "You are gods."
- Romans 8:14-19, 29 - The sons of God are being brought to glory and conformed to the image of the Son.
- Hebrews 2:10-11 - Many sons are brought into glory, and the sanctified share one origin.
- 1 John 3:1-2 - We will be like Him, because we will see Him as He is.

[3] In life.
- 1 John 5:11-12 - God gave us eternal life, and this life is in His Son.
- John 10:10 - I came that they may have life and have it abundantly.

[4] In nature.
- 2 Peter 1:4 - We become partakers of the divine nature.
- Ephesians 4:24 - The new man is created according to God in righteousness and holiness.

[5] But not in the Godhead.
- Mark 12:29 - The Lord our God, the Lord is one.
- Deuteronomy 6:4 - Hear, O Israel: The Lord is one.
- Isaiah 45:5 - I am the Lord, and there is no other.
